# Daily Educational Video Pipeline

## Purpose and Format

This internal workflow prepares one **60-second, 9:16 vertical, Hindi-English educational video** per day for a review queue. The rotation covers neuroscience, everyday diet literacy, and mental-health literacy. Every production remains educational, evidence-led, and suitable for a general audience; it must not diagnose, prescribe treatment, promise outcomes, or replace professional care.

## Daily Topic Rotation

| Rotation day | Topic family | Example safe angle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Neuroscience | A simple explanation of attention, learning, sleep, or stress physiology without overclaiming. |
| Day 2 | Diet literacy | Food patterns, label-reading, hydration, or balanced-meal concepts without individualized diet prescriptions. |
| Day 3 | Mental-health literacy | Coping skills, help-seeking, sleep hygiene, or stigma reduction without diagnosis or crisis counseling. |

Then repeat the cycle. A topic may be skipped when its evidence base is weak, conflicting, or too nuanced for a 60-second educational format.

## Evidence Standard

1. Start with an official public-health, university, or government health source whenever available.
2. Add a peer-reviewed systematic review, clinical guideline, or primary study only when it is directly relevant and its limits can be stated accurately.
3. Expert talks, books, and YouTube lectures may inspire an angle but are not enough on their own for a factual claim.
4. Record source title, organization or authors, publication date, URL, claim used, and a short limitation note in the internal research ledger.
5. Do not use sensational, deterministic, or “brain hack” claims. Use calibrated language such as “can support,” “is associated with,” or “research suggests” where appropriate.

## 60-Second Production Standard

| Element | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Canvas | 9:16 vertical, 1080 × 1920 target, upright orientation. |
| Language | Hindi-English mix, plain spoken words, technical terms explained once. |
| Script length | Target 105–125 spoken words, with a one-sentence source or limitation cue where needed. |
| Narrative arc | Hook (0–5s), evidence-led explanation (5–40s), practical non-medical takeaway (40–55s), source/disclaimer cue (55–60s). |
| Visual direction | Calm editorial-science aesthetic: warm neutral background, restrained teal/amber accents, simple anatomical or lifestyle metaphors, no sensational medical imagery. |
| Captions | High-contrast, short Hindi-English phrases; captions must not distort or add claims absent from the narration. |
| Music | Optional unobtrusive instrumental bed, mixed below narration. |

## Face and Voice Reference Boundary

Use only user-authorized original voice and full-face reference material. Before any identity-consistent generation or edit, label the specific source file and permitted use in the asset ledger. Do not use a third party’s voice, face, clip, identity, or copyrighted footage as a substitute. If an authorized high-quality face or voice source is unavailable, create a faceless visual draft and mark the presentation asset as pending.

## Internal Daily Workflow

1. Select the next rotation topic and research an evidence-led angle.
2. Create a fact ledger and a 60-second bilingual script draft.
3. Create a scene plan, visual-reference requirements, caption plan, and voice-reference checklist.
4. Generate or edit only internal draft assets after the relevant source and identity checks pass.
5. Save the script, source ledger, and asset checklist to the internal review queue.
6. Prepare platform-specific title, description, source note, and hashtags as drafts only.
7. Do not publish, message, share files externally, or pay for boosts. A final platform-specific confirmation remains required before any public post.

## Publishing Review Queue

Each completed daily package must contain:

- Topic, audience, and educational purpose.
- Source ledger and claim limitations.
- Final narration script and visible caption text.
- Scene plan and asset provenance list.
- Voice/face reference status.
- Draft title, caption, source note, and platform target.
- Explicit state: `READY FOR REVIEW — NOT PUBLISHED`.
