# Reel_0002 — Work interruptions and task switching

## Verified source base

| Source | Claim allowed in the Reel | Limitation recorded |
|---|---|---|
| American Psychological Association, *Multitasking: Switching costs* | Task-switching experiments show time costs; goal shifting and rule activation are useful simplified descriptions of executive-control stages. | APA synthesis describes experimental findings; it does not measure this viewer’s productivity or health. |
| Rick et al., 2024, *Work* (PMC10789356) | In a one-day diary study of 492 German office workers, the association between interruption frequency and subjective workload was mediated by interruption overload; task complexity moderated the relationship. | Observational diary design, German full-time office workers, subjective-workload outcome; no causal claims about an individual worker. |
| Madore & Wagner, 2019, *Cerebrum* (PMC7075496) | Task switching places demands on control and attention systems; behavioural switch costs can show slower speed or lower accuracy relative to task repetition. | General scientific overview; no diagnosis, brain scan inference, or fixed effect size should be stated. |

## Script boundaries

The script says `may`, `often`, and `context matters`. It does not call notifications harmful, recommend clinical treatment, promise productivity, or say the viewer has an attention deficit. The practical example is a voluntary work-design experiment: one priority task, a short focus window, and a planned check point.
