# Reel_0003 — Home attention reset after distraction

## Source classification

| Source | Classification | Claim allowed | Recorded limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| American Psychological Association, *Multitasking: Switching costs* | Scientific evidence summary | Switching tasks can cost time; task complexity and familiarity affect observed switching costs. | Synthesis of experimental findings; it does not predict one viewer’s performance at home. |
| Draheim et al., 2022, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* (PMC8853083) | Peer-reviewed review / expert interpretation | Attention control concerns maintaining goal-relevant behaviour and filtering or blocking irrelevant information amid distraction. | Review of attention-control constructs and individual differences; not a clinical assessment. |
| Middlebrooks et al., 2017, *Psychological Science* (PMC5546942) | Peer-reviewed experimental evidence | In this laboratory study, an active divided-attention task reduced overall recall relative to full attention; the tested background-music conditions did not show the same overall recall impairment. | Undergraduate sample and specific tasks; results do not mean all home sound, music, or notifications affect every person the same way. |

## Claim boundaries

The Reel calls the method a voluntary “reset prompt,” not treatment. It does not say that home distractions cause a disorder, that silence is always necessary, or that the method will improve memory or productivity.
