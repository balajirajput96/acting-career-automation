## Evidence Ledger and Source Documentation

To ensure rigorous scientific and public health grounding, the content is synthesized from four authoritative institutions. Each source provides empirical backing for the nutritional benefits and dietary integration of whole grains.

| Source Organization | Document / Reference Title | Supported Claim | URL / Access |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health** | The Nutrition Source: Whole Grains | Whole grains provide a complete package of dietary fiber, B vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants that support metabolic health and reduce chronic disease risk compared to refined grains. | [Harvard Nutrition Source](https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/what-should-you-eat/whole-grains/) [1] |
| **Mayo Clinic** | Whole grains: Hearty options for a healthy diet | Incorporating whole grains into daily eating patterns assists in regulating cholesterol levels, stabilizing blood pressure, and supporting healthy digestion. | [Mayo Clinic Guide](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/nutrition-and-healthy-eating/in-depth/whole-grains/art-20047826) [2] |
| **ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition (NIN)** | Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2024) | National dietary guidelines recommend preferring whole grains, millets, and sprouted grains as staple components of traditional Indian meals to enhance dietary fiber and micronutrient intake. | [NIN Guidelines](https://www.nin.res.in/dietaryguidelines/pdfjs/locale/DGI_2024.pdf) [3] |
| **World Health Organization** | Healthy Diet Fact Sheet | Unprocessed whole grains such as millet, oats, maize, and brown rice protect against malnutrition and non-communicable diseases as part of a balanced diet. | [WHO Fact Sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet) [4] |

> **Synthesized Evidence Summary:** Authoritative public health and academic bodies consistently emphasize that transitioning from refined grains to whole-grain alternatives improves nutritional profile, aids glycemic control, and supports cardiovascular and digestive wellness.

## Limitations and Safety Framework

The educational package strictly adheres to public health communication boundaries to ensure absolute safety and regulatory compliance. 

The content is intended **strictly for general educational and informational purposes**. Under no circumstances does this material diagnose medical or metabolic conditions, prescribe nutritional therapy or clinical interventions, promise specific health outcomes, claim cures for illnesses, provide individualized dietary counseling, or substitute for professional medical care and registered dietitian consultations. Viewers presenting with specific health conditions, metabolic disorders, or dietary restrictions are explicitly advised to seek guidance from qualified healthcare practitioners. Furthermore, all promotional or external publishing language has been strictly omitted.

## Hindi-English Narration Script

The following script is meticulously crafted for neutral faceless narration, maintaining an exact length of **125 words** to fit comfortably within the 60-second pacing window.

> Whole grains like brown rice, millets, and whole wheat are essential parts of a balanced diet. Aaj hum baat karenge kaise hum familiar daily meals mein whole grains ko easily include kar sakte hain. Refined grains ke mukable, whole grains mein fiber, B vitamins, aur minerals jyada hote hain jo digestion aur overall health ko support karte hain. Aap apne regular rotis mein wheat flour ke sath thoda bajra, jowar, ya ragi mix kar sakte hain. Subah ke nashte mein oats ya daliya choose karna bhi ek accha option hai. Chote badlav jaise white rice ki jagah brown rice ya millets use karna daily nutrition ko boost karta hai. Yeh educational guidance hai, koi medical prescription nahi. Balance aur variety maintain karna sabse zaroori hai.
