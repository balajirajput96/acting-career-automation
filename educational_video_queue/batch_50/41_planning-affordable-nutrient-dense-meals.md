   - **Organization:** Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health (The Nutrition Source)
   - **URL:** https://nutritionsource.hsph.harvard.edu/strategies-nutrition-budget/
   - **Precise Supported Claim:** Eating well on a budget is achievable by prioritizing affordable pantry staples like beans, lentils, and whole grains, alongside strategic meal planning.

3. **Source Title:** Access to Foods That Support Healthy Dietary Patterns
   - **Organization:** Office of Disease Prevention and Health Promotion (ODPHP / Healthy People)

   - **Precise Supported Claim:** Healthy dietary patterns rely on consistent access to affordable, nutrient-dense forms of foods and beverages across all food groups within recommended limits.

4. **Source Title:** Nutrition on a Budget
   - **Organization:** USDA Food and Nutrition Service (Nutrition.gov)
   - **URL:** https://www.nutrition.gov/topics/food-security-and-access/nutrition-budget
   - **Precise Supported Claim:** Advance meal planning, smart grocery shopping, and utilizing low-cost nutrient-rich foods help maintain balanced nutrition without excessive expenditure.

## 4. Hindi-English Narration Script (114 Words)
"Namaste! Good nutrition does not require expensive ingredients or imported superfoods. Developing diet literacy empowers you to plan affordable, nutrient-dense meals right at home. 

First, prioritize accessible kitchen staples like lentils, whole grains, beans, and seasonal vegetables. These provide essential fiber, plant protein, and vitamins at a fraction of the cost. 

Second, plan your weekly grocery list and prepare meals in advance. This prevents impulse buying, lowers grocery bills, and reduces food waste. Buying local, seasonal produce also cuts expenses while maximizing nutrient freshness. 

Mindful adjustments to how you shop and cook make balanced eating sustainable for every household. Always consult a qualified nutrition professional for personalized dietary guidance. Eat well and stay healthy!"
